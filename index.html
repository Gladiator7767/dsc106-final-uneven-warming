Where does the scenario gap grow?

The scenario difference here is the result of subtracting SSP1-2.6 from SSP5-8.5 within the same region, period, and model. It compares simulated outcomes under two different assumptions. It does not represent a probability of occurrence, nor can it be directly interpreted as the effect of a specific policy.

04 / Look at the results of the different models

How close are the estimates from the three models for the same region?

We saw earlier that global average warming is not representative of every region. In the "Mean of three models" view, the numerical value displayed is the average of the results of the three climate models. Here you keep the region and time period you selected, and look at the three models separately to give you an idea of how different the model estimates are behind the average warming in that region.

The range listed below the figure shows the lowest and highest estimates for the very high emissions scenario for the three models. It is not a confidence interval and there is no guarantee that the future true outcome will necessarily fall into it. We chose these three models because we had all the data we needed; they do not cover all climate models or all possible futures.

YOUR TAKEAWAY

A global number
is only the beginning.

The global average is useful, but it does not represent the results for every region. By comparing different scenarios, periods, and models along the same latitudinal band, these differences become apparent. When reading a warming value, it's also important to consider where it applies and what assumptions were used.

Read the numbers and their limits

These are climate simulations under specific conditions, not observed future temperatures. The average for a latitudinal band also masks differences within the band. This comparison describes the data we selected and does not tell us how much a specific location will warm, nor does it assign probabilities to either scenario.
