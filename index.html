First, let's look at the global picture. Among the three models we selected, the average results of the two scenarios will clearly diverge by the end of the century. The global average provides us with a reference, but it doesn't tell us whether these temperature increases are uniform across different regions.

Both lines use the same temperature baseline and the same models. This is a comparison of conditional simulations, not two forecasts with assigned probabilities.

You can try switching between the early, middle, and late periods of the century. Comparing averages over a period makes it easier to discern long-term trends than focusing on just one year. Short-term fluctuations may still bring the two scenarios close together or alter the order of high and low values in certain years and models.

Where does the scenario gap grow?

The scenario difference here is the result of subtracting SSP1-2.6 from SSP5-8.5 within the same region, period, and model. It compares simulated outcomes under two different assumptions. It does not represent a probability of occurrence, nor can it be directly interpreted as the effect of a specific policy.

Select a cell to change both region and period. The matrix uses three fixed 20-year periods; a custom period is shown in the charts above.

04 / LOOK BEHIND THE MEAN

One mean.
Three different answers.

The average is just a starting point. To see how much the results differ, we need to examine the models individually. When comparing two scenarios within the same model, we don't mix low-emission results from one model with high-emission results from another.

The range here represents the minimum and maximum values among the three models. We selected these three models because the required data was complete. This range is not a confidence interval, nor does it include all CMIP6 models or all possible futures.

YOUR TAKEAWAY

A global number
is only the beginning.

The global average is useful, but it does not represent the results for every region. By comparing different scenarios, periods, and models along the same latitudinal band, these differences become apparent. When reading a warming value, it's also important to consider where it applies and what assumptions were used.

Read the numbers and their limits

These are climate simulations under specific conditions, not observed future temperatures. The average for a latitudinal band also masks differences within the band. This comparison describes the data we selected and does not tell us how much a specific location will warm, nor does it assign probabilities to either scenario.
